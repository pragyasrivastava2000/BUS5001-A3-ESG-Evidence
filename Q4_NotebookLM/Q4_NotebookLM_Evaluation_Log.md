
# Q4 — NotebookLM Experiment Evaluation Log

**Subject:** BUS5001 — Cloud Platforms and Analytics  
**Assessment:** Assessment 3, Question 4  
**Platform:** Google NotebookLM  
**Scenario:** University research and revision on IFRS S1 and IFRS S2

---

## 1. Evaluation Purpose

This log documents seven practical experiments conducted to evaluate Google NotebookLM as an academic research and learning assistant.

The evaluation considers:

- Functionality and usability
- Accuracy and relevance
- Usefulness in academic workflows
- Evidence quality and source traceability
- Limitations, hallucination risks and over-reliance

## 2. Source Materials

Two official IFRS Foundation overview webpages were imported:

1. [IFRS S1 — General Requirements](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s1-general-requirements/)
2. [IFRS S2 — Climate-related Disclosures](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s2-climate-related-disclosures/)

**Source limitation:** These are overview webpages rather than the complete technical standards.

## E01 — Source Import

### Objective

Test NotebookLM's ability to import and organise official research materials.

### Procedure

1. Created a NotebookLM workspace.
2. Added the official IFRS S1 overview webpage.
3. Added the official IFRS S2 overview webpage.
4. Confirmed that both sources were available.

### Observed Output

Both sources were successfully imported.

### Evaluation

**Strength:** Source import was straightforward and supported a focused research workspace.

**Limitation:** The source collection did not include the full text of either standard.

**Academic Usefulness:** Useful for preparing and organising research material.

**Result:** Successful.

---

## E02 — Source-Grounded Question Answering

### Objective

Evaluate the relevance and accuracy of answers generated from the selected sources.

### Procedure

NotebookLM was asked to explain the four core disclosure areas under IFRS S1.

### Observed Output

The response identified:

1. Governance
2. Strategy
3. Risk Management
4. Metrics and Targets

NotebookLM also provided explanations of the disclosure areas.

### Evaluation

**Strength:** The response was structured, relevant and suitable for introductory learning.

**Limitation:** Clickable source citations were not visible in the copied response evidence.

**Academic Usefulness:** Helpful for understanding terminology and clarifying concepts.

**Result:** Relevant response generated; source traceability was limited in the captured output.

---

## E03 — Academic Summarisation

### Objective

Evaluate NotebookLM's ability to prepare a concise academic summary.

### Procedure

Requested an approximately 200-word summary covering IFRS S1 and IFRS S2.

### Observed Output

NotebookLM generated a summary of approximately 230 words covering:

- Purpose of IFRS S1 and IFRS S2
- Four core disclosure areas
- Sustainability and climate reporting
- Relevance to organisational reporting

### Evaluation

**Strength:** Consolidated information into a coherent academic explanation.

**Limitations:**

- Slightly exceeded the requested word count.
- Direct citations were not visible in the copied output.
- Some statements about mandatory application required qualification.

**Academic Usefulness:** Helpful for preparing an initial research briefing.

**Result:** Successful, with minor instruction-following and verification limitations.

---

## E04 — Cross-Source Comparison

### Objective

Evaluate NotebookLM's ability to compare two related standards.

### Procedure

Requested a comparison of IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 across seven dimensions.

### Observed Output

NotebookLM generated a structured comparison distinguishing:

- General sustainability disclosures
- Climate-related disclosures
- Reporting scope
- Core disclosure areas
- Climate-related risks
- Implementation considerations
- Professional implications

### Evaluation

**Strength:** Supported comparative learning and highlighted the relationship between the standards.

**Limitations:**

- Some mandatory-adoption wording was overgeneralised.
- Certain technical references required verification.
- Direct citations were not visible in the copied output.

**Academic Usefulness:** Useful for developing a comparison table and research briefing.

**Result:** Successful, subject to technical verification.

## E06 — Quiz Generation

### Objective

Evaluate NotebookLM's ability to generate assessment questions and explanatory feedback.

### Procedure

Generated a university-level multiple-choice quiz comparing IFRS S1 and IFRS S2.

### Observed Output

NotebookLM generated **10 multiple-choice questions**.

Each question presented four answer options.

The interactive feedback displayed correct answers and explanations.

### Selected Answer Verification

| Question | Topic | Correct Option | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | IFRS S1 versus IFRS S2 scope | A | Consistent with IFRS concepts |
| Q2 | Early application | C | Consistent with IFRS concepts |
| Q3 | Four disclosure areas | B | Consistent with IFRS concepts |
| Q5 | Physical and transition risks | D | Consistent with IFRS concepts |

This is a selected-sample finding, not a claim that all ten questions were independently verified.

- Paragraph-level citations were not visible.
- Verification covered five selected questions.

**Academic Usefulness:** Suitable for formative assessment and revision.

**Result:** Successful generation and sample evaluation.

## E07 — Mind Map Generation

### Objective

Evaluate NotebookLM's ability to organise research concepts visually.

### Procedure

Generated a comparative mind map using both IFRS sources.

### Observed Output

The mind map contained four main branches:

1. IFRS S1 — General Requirements
2. IFRS S2 — Climate-related Disclosures
3. Four Core Disclosure Areas
4. Integration and Relationship

The map included subtopics covering scope, risks, opportunities, disclosure requirements and the relationship between the standards.

### Evaluation

**Strengths:**

- Organised information hierarchically.
- Included all four disclosure pillars.
- Clearly distinguished IFRS S1 and IFRS S2.
- Supported visual learning.

**Limitations:**

- Technical concepts were simplified.
- Direct paragraph-level citations were absent.
- Cross-branch relationships were limited.
- Effective-date wording required contextual interpretation.

**Academic Usefulness:** Useful for concept mapping and exam preparation.

**Result:** Successful.

| Reliability | Technical and regulatory statements required verification |
| Limitations | Source dependence, simplification and potential over-reliance |

## 5. Feasibility Recommendation

NotebookLM is suitable for a controlled university pilot as a supplementary academic research and revision assistant.

Its strongest demonstrated uses include:

- Organising research sources
- Explaining introductory concepts
- Preparing summaries and comparisons
- Generating flashcards and quizzes
- Creating visual concept maps

University adoption should include guidance on source verification, privacy, appropriate use and human oversight.

NotebookLM should support, rather than replace, critical reading and independent evaluation of academic sources.

## 8. Conclusion

The seven experiments demonstrated NotebookLM's ability to support different stages of an academic research workflow.

Its strengths were particularly evident in source organisation, summarisation, interactive revision and visual learning.

However, incomplete source material, technical simplification and limited citation visibility in some captured outputs highlighted the need for independent verification.

The findings support the use of NotebookLM as a supplementary academic assistant rather than an authoritative replacement for original research sources.
