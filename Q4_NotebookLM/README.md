
# Q4 — Evaluation of Google NotebookLM as an Academic Research Assistant

**Subject:** BUS5001 — Cloud Platforms and Analytics  
**Assessment:** Assessment 3, Question 4  
**Technology:** Google NotebookLM  
**Scenario:** Academic Research on IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 Sustainability Disclosure Standards

---

## 1. Project Overview

This project evaluates Google NotebookLM as a cloud-based, AI-powered research and learning assistant for university students.

The evaluation uses a simulated academic scenario in which a student prepares an evidence-based briefing on sustainability reporting requirements under IFRS S1 and IFRS S2.

The investigation examines NotebookLM's ability to organise research sources, answer questions, generate academic summaries, compare information and produce interactive study materials.

Seven practical experiments were conducted to evaluate functionality, relevance, accuracy, academic usefulness and limitations.

## 2. Academic Scenario

### Research Topic

**Understanding and Comparing IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 Sustainability Disclosure Standards**

The simulated student needs to:

1. Understand the purpose and scope of IFRS S1 and IFRS S2.
2. Identify the four core disclosure areas.
3. Compare general sustainability and climate-related disclosure requirements.
4. Prepare a concise academic briefing.
5. Revise important concepts using interactive study tools.
6. Organise the relationship between the two standards visually.

NotebookLM was evaluated as a potential support tool for these activities.

## 3. Research Sources

Two official IFRS Foundation overview webpages were imported into NotebookLM.

### Source 1 — IFRS S1

[IFRS S1 — General Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s1-general-requirements/)

### Source 2 — IFRS S2

[IFRS S2 — Climate-related Disclosures](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s2-climate-related-disclosures/)

**Source limitation:** The selected webpages provide official introductory information but do not reproduce the complete technical text of the standards.

This distinction was considered when evaluating the depth and reliability of generated outputs.

## 6. Experiment Results

### Experiment 1 — Source Import

**Objective:** Assess whether NotebookLM could ingest the two official IFRS Foundation webpages.

**Method:** Both source URLs were added to a dedicated NotebookLM workspace.

**Observed Result:** Both sources were successfully imported and made available for subsequent activities.

**Strength:** Sources could be organised within a single research workspace.

**Limitation:** The available research content was restricted to the imported overview pages rather than the complete IFRS standards.

**Evaluation:** Successful source import established the foundation for source-based academic activities.

### Experiment 2 — Source-Grounded Question Answering

**Objective:** Evaluate NotebookLM's ability to answer questions about IFRS disclosure requirements.

**Method:** Questions were submitted about IFRS S1, including its four core disclosure areas.

**Observed Result:** NotebookLM identified:

1. Governance
2. Strategy
3. Risk Management
4. Metrics and Targets

It also generated explanations of the disclosure areas.

**Strength:** Responses were relevant and organised around the requested concepts.

**Limitation:** Direct clickable citations were not visible in the copied response evidence, limiting independent source traceability.

**Evaluation:** The feature was useful for introductory academic research, but important technical statements still required source verification.

### Experiment 3 — Academic Summarisation

**Objective:** Evaluate the usefulness of AI-generated summaries for preparing a university briefing.

**Method:** NotebookLM was prompted to produce an approximately 200-word academic summary of IFRS S1 and IFRS S2.

**Observed Result:** The generated summary was approximately 230 words and covered the standards' purposes, disclosure areas and reporting relevance.

**Strength:** The summary consolidated key concepts into a coherent academic explanation.

**Limitations:**

- The response exceeded the requested approximate word count.
- Detailed source citations were not visible in the copied output.
- Some statements about mandatory application required jurisdictional qualification.

**Evaluation:** The summary was useful as a research starting point but required review before academic use.

### Experiment 4 — Cross-Source Comparison

**Objective:** Assess whether NotebookLM could compare IFRS S1 and IFRS S2.

**Method:** The tool was asked to compare the standards across seven dimensions and identify professional implications.

**Observed Result:** NotebookLM distinguished the general sustainability disclosure focus of IFRS S1 from the climate-specific requirements of IFRS S2.

**Strength:** The structured comparison helped clarify how the standards complement each other.

**Limitations:**

- Some wording about mandatory adoption was overgeneralised.
- Technical references required checking against authoritative materials.
- Direct source citations were not visible in the copied comparison.

**Evaluation:** Useful for comparative learning, with source verification necessary for regulatory or technical conclusions.

### Experiment 5 — Flashcard Generation

**Objective:** Evaluate NotebookLM as a revision and self-study tool.

**Method:** Flashcards were generated using the imported IFRS sources.

**Observed Result:** NotebookLM produced **55 flashcards** covering sustainability reporting concepts and related terminology.

A sample of the generated flashcards was reviewed.

**Strengths:**

- Supported active recall and independent revision.
- Converted research material into short question-and-answer items.
- Covered a range of conceptual and technical topics.

**Limitations:**

- Not all 55 flashcards were individually reviewed.
- Some technical details required verification.
- Source citations were not clearly visible in the reviewed flashcard screenshots.

**Evaluation:** Useful for revision, but technical accuracy should not be assumed across the complete flashcard set.

### Experiment 6 — Quiz Generation

**Objective:** Evaluate NotebookLM's ability to create academic assessment questions and interactive feedback.

**Method:** A university-level multiple-choice quiz was generated from the two IFRS sources.

**Observed Result:** NotebookLM generated **10 multiple-choice questions**.

The quiz included answer options, correct-answer feedback and explanations.

Five selected questions were reviewed for accuracy:

| Question | Topic | Verification Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 scope | Consistent with official IFRS concepts |
| Q2 | Early application requirements | Consistent with official IFRS concepts |
| Q3 | Four core disclosure areas | Consistent with official IFRS concepts |
| Q5 | Physical and transition climate risks | Consistent with official IFRS concepts |
| Q10 | Relationship between IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 | Consistent with official IFRS concepts |

**Sample Result:** 5 out of 5 selected answers were consistent with the relevant IFRS concepts.

This is a sample verification result, **not a claim of 100% accuracy across the complete quiz**.

**Strengths:**

- Generated relevant multiple-choice questions.
- Provided immediate feedback on selected answers.
- Explained correct and incorrect answer options.
- Supported independent formative assessment.

**Limitations:**

- Questions mainly assessed conceptual recall.
- Some distractors were relatively easy to eliminate.
- Direct paragraph-level source citations were not visible.
- The five-question sample did not establish accuracy across every question.

**Evaluation:** Effective for introductory revision and self-assessment, but not a replacement for formal source verification.

### Experiment 7 — Mind Map Generation

**Objective:** Evaluate NotebookLM's ability to organise academic concepts visually.

**Method:** A comparative mind map was generated using both IFRS sources.

**Observed Result:** The mind map contained four main branches:

1. IFRS S1
2. IFRS S2
3. Four Core Disclosure Areas
4. Integration and Relationship

**Strengths:**

- Clearly separated the two standards.
- Included all four core disclosure areas.
- Organised concepts into a hierarchical structure.
- Supported visual revision and conceptual understanding.

**Limitations:**

- Technical requirements were simplified.
- Specific IFRS paragraph citations were not displayed.
- Relationships between branches were less detailed than a full regulatory analysis.
- The effective date required clarification to avoid implying universal mandatory adoption.

**Evaluation:** Useful for organising introductory concepts, but insufficient as a standalone technical reference.

## 7. Overall Critical Evaluation

### 7.1 Accuracy and Relevance

NotebookLM generally produced relevant content aligned with the selected IFRS research topic.

The five selected quiz answers were consistent with the relevant IFRS concepts.

However, technical outputs still required verification, particularly where statements concerned reporting obligations, effective dates or detailed disclosure requirements.

### 7.2 Usefulness in Academic Workflows

| Academic Activity | NotebookLM Capability | Observed Usefulness |
|---|---|---|
| Research preparation | Source Import | Organised research materials |
| Concept clarification | Question Answering | Explained key disclosure concepts |
| Briefing preparation | Summarisation | Condensed source material |
| Comparative analysis | Cross-Source Comparison | Highlighted differences and relationships |
| Exam preparation | Flashcards | Supported active recall |
| Self-assessment | Quiz | Provided interactive feedback |
| Visual learning | Mind Map | Organised concepts hierarchically |

### 7.3 Limitations and Risks

**Source Dependence:** Outputs are constrained by the completeness and quality of imported material.

**Hallucination Risk:** Plausible technical explanations may contain unsupported details or overgeneralised claims.

**Evidence Quality:** Not all exported or copied outputs displayed direct source citations.

**Regulatory Interpretation:** IFRS effective dates should not be confused with mandatory adoption in every jurisdiction.

**Over-Reliance:** Students may accept generated summaries or answers without consulting authoritative sources.

**Technical Depth:** Simplified educational outputs may omit important reporting requirements or exceptions.

**Verification Effort:** High-stakes claims require additional checking, reducing the time savings of automated research.

## 8. Feasibility for University Adoption

NotebookLM demonstrated potential value as a supplementary academic learning tool.

Its strongest applications in this evaluation were:

- Organising source materials.
- Explaining foundational concepts.
- Producing study summaries and comparisons.
- Supporting revision through flashcards and quizzes.
- Visualising relationships between research topics.

However, university adoption should consider:

1. Student training in source verification.
2. Clear guidance on appropriate AI-assisted academic use.
3. Privacy and confidentiality of uploaded materials.
4. Accessibility and equitable access.
5. Human oversight for technical or high-stakes academic content.
6. The limitations of incomplete or outdated source collections.

**Recommendation:** NotebookLM is suitable for a controlled educational pilot as a supplementary research and revision assistant, provided that source verification, privacy safeguards and appropriate human oversight are maintained.

## 10. Conclusion

The seven experiments demonstrated that Google NotebookLM can support multiple stages of university research and learning.

The tool was particularly effective at transforming selected source material into summaries, comparisons, interactive study activities and visual knowledge structures.

Nevertheless, source limitations, inconsistent citation visibility, technical simplification and the risk of unsupported statements restrict its suitability as an authoritative research source.

The evaluation supports using NotebookLM as a **supplementary academic assistant rather than a substitute for critical reading, source verification or independent academic judgement**.
